It was a Thursday evening (earlier this year) and I decided it was time to buy a new laptop as the 2015 Dell w/ an i7 5600U just wasn't cutting it anymore. I spent _way_ too much time trying to figure out what to buy and settled on an M5 Macbook Air after Apple had announced that yes, due to the RAM-pocalypse going on, they would be [raising prices](https://daringfireball.net/linked/2026/06/22/apple-device-prices-when) as well. I had decided already that I wanted a machine with long battery life and that would last a decade, so that was enough for me to pull the trigger. It would arrive nearly a week later, after the estimate being a mere two days. As you may be able to tell, this is not a review of my Macbook Air M5. No, this is a tale involving FedEx & bad software. I had long been considering writing this but I didn't want to write a hit piece on a company. It would not be until an even more recent hassle involving FedEx just last week that I would decide to tell this story. But first, the first incident.

It all started with a very simple email: your package has been shipped and should be delivered on Monday, June 22nd and a signature would be required. I would plan to work from home that morning and get the package, not a problem. My workplace is flexible (though not all could be, more on that later). Saturday rolls around; nobody emails but I check my phone in the morning and reflexively refresh the tracking page. Package is going to be delivered today instead. Ah... I have plans so I suppose I'll simply ask them to hold it at the station or something. This way I can go pick it up on Monday, even easier, right? Well, the website would not let me do that. Every time I would click "Manage Delivery" I would just get an internal error. Contact customer service. I'll skip the details: long story short I was told they would send it to the pick up point and there should be no issue. The driver **would give me a call** if anything went wrong. Day is done and off to bed, then.

Sunday rolls around: reflexively refresh the tracking page. "Your package is out for delivery" is written with no information. The page still indicates the drop off will be my home. I guess I should stay at home all day? Well, let's just be sure and give them a call...! The phone menu would send me down a maze of many options. It would refuse to let me talk to a human until I had completed all it's screening questions. At one point during the screening, I got something akin to "And just so you know, you can track your package using our self-serve tools on our web- I am sorry, an internal error has occurred. Please hang up and try your call again.". Right, time to call back and start again. Eventually, I get a human.

"Ah yes, I see that there is a note. The driver will deliver it today to your house today.". Anyway, the day went by and nothing was delivered. The driver never did call me but the depot sure did to let me know that their drivers don't carry phones and customer service had misinformed me. Also, they don't deliver on Sundays. They let me know on Monday, that is. It turns out that Best Buy did not authorize the drop off to the pick up point and wanted in-person signatures, so my request was simply blackholed despite a rep telling me it would be honoured. The website never made this clear at any point. The request was simply eaten from my perspective. Same with the note about calling. So I reflexively refresh the tracking page and...
"Invalid date"! Nice, some Javascript parsing gone wrong probably. At this point I was going to be having a busy week and needed to be in the office, so having it delivered on a Tuesday or Wednesday while they sorted that was out would be annoying.

Time for a side bar rant that isn't a software issue: I called Best Buy and simply asked if they could return the package and I would buy it in store. A 20 minute trip would save all this trouble. They refused and claimed it was impossible and heavily discouraged me from refusing the package. That would impact their bottom line, I am sure. I probably should have let them suffer the consequences of the carrier they picked but I've had a fairly good shopping experience with them, so I gave them a pass here and asked them to release the package to FedEx. To make a long story short, it took a couple days to work that out but eventually the package was sent for pick up. The app didn't show this update by the way, just an email I got. That's about good as I can ask for at this point.

What went wrong?

- I had zero feedback or visibility into the entire pick-up and drop off thing. They are storing some bits on their side obviously for this (hopefully!) and yet it's opaque and lossy to me.
- Apparently their app has zero knowledge that their station doesn't work Sundays. How is this a thing?
- The phone system can just terminate the entire call when it decides that a pre-recorded finite state machine blew up? Really?
- Their self serve tools error. "Invalid Date". Really?

This whole thing probably took 3 hours wall clock time for me to chase down. A few other things went wrong and I cannot be bothered to write it all down. One thing is pretty obvious, I should have just bought it in person. I should have just refused the package. Software and modern conveniences were supposed to make my life easier and yet they sucked up non-trivial amounts of my weekend to resolve their glossed over problems. The thing is: we have LLM solving millennium prize problems, a PS5 emulator advancing at a rapid pace and the claims of LLM models one-shotting very impressive pieces of software. I've written a handful of wild things myself, too. So why do software and systems still suck? I know the real reason is boring and trite: "software is hard, legacy systems even more so". My new Macbook Air has had it's share of at least a dozen bugs since unboxing it. Software is definitely not yet solved. And yet, we wonder every month if it is. There are dozens of blogs all around the web right now proclaiming this very thing. The front page stories of Hacker News often have their comment sections [littered with comments to this tune](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48955074).

Oh, and by the way: I ordered another laptop from Wisetek recently had a new set of bugs on their tracking UI hindering self serve. In this instance, it had told me that my request to the pick up point had been rejected because the sender required a signature. I didn't have time to deal with it and planned to address it later. The package ended up being sent to the pick up point the next day anyway. The UI never did update.

So, what will it take to display some tracking information on a page correctly? Maybe we'll cure cancer first. After all, the capital to start a new package delivery company may give such companies a moat wide enough to simply do nothing and win. Perhaps, that is the problem. As for me, well I guess this is why Amazon keeps getting my money. I can't say I really ever have any trouble and I order probably dozens of packages a year.

C'est la vie.
